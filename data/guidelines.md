### [RULE-FALL-01] Unwitnessed Fall & Anticoagulant Protocol
If a resident experiences an unwitnessed fall or strikes their head, record vital signs and neurological checks (pupil reactivity, orientation) every 30 minutes for 2 hours. Immediate physician notification is required if the resident is taking anticoagulants (e.g., Eliquis, apixaban, warfarin, Xarelto) or shows dizziness, confusion, or nausea.

### [RULE-HYD-02] Acute Dehydration & Low Fluid Intake
For residents presenting with dry mucous membranes, dark concentrated urine, or lethargy alongside oral fluid intake below 800 ml over 24 hours, initiate hourly small-volume oral hydration (100 ml/hr) and start a 24-hour fluid balance chart. Escalate to the nursing supervisor if blood pressure falls below 90/60 mmHg.

### [RULE-SKIN-03] Stage 1 Pressure Injury Prevention
Non-blanchable erythema (redness that does not fade when pressed) over a bony prominence (sacrum, heels, hips) indicates a Stage 1 pressure injury. Reposition the resident every 2 hours, apply a protective hydrocolloid barrier dressing, and document skin integrity at each shift change. Never massage reddened areas.

### [RULE-MED-04] Missed Oral Medication Window
If a scheduled oral maintenance medication is missed by more than 2 hours, do not double the next dose. Consult the medication administration record (MAR) guidelines and contact the on-call pharmacist for narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (e.g., digoxin, levothyroxine, warfarin, anticonvulsants).

### [RULE-GLUC-05] Mild-to-Moderate Hypoglycemia (Rule of 15)
If a conscious resident exhibits shakiness, diaphoresis (sweating), or confusion with blood glucose between 54 and 69 mg/dL, administer 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates (e.g., 4 oz fruit juice or glucose tablets) and recheck blood glucose in 15 minutes. Repeat once if glucose remains below 70 mg/dL.

### [RULE-RESP-06] Acute Dyspnea & Hypoxia Escalation
If a resident develops acute shortness of breath (dyspnea), accessory muscle use, or resting oxygen saturation (SpO2) below 92% (or below 88% for documented COPD), elevate the head of the bed to 45 degrees, check vital signs immediately, and notify the registered nurse or physician for oxygen therapy orders.

### [RULE-UTI-07] Suspected Urinary Tract Infection Screening
New-onset dysuria (painful urination), urinary frequency, suprapubic tenderness, or sudden unexplained acute confusion (delirium) in an older adult warrants a clean-catch urinalysis order and temperature check. Encourage oral fluids unless fluid-restricted and notify the primary provider with vital signs.

### [RULE-DYSP-08] Swallowing Difficulty & Aspiration Precautions
If a resident coughs, chokes, or exhibits a wet/gurgly voice during or immediately after meals, pause oral intake immediately, keep the resident upright at 90 degrees for at least 30 minutes, inspect the oral cavity for pocketing, and request a speech-language pathology (SLP) swallowing evaluation before resuming thin liquids.

### [RULE-PAIN-09] Acute Uncontrolled Pain Reassessment
When a resident reports severe acute pain (score 7 or higher on a 0–10 numeric scale) or non-verbal pain indicators (guarding, grimacing), assess location, onset, and vital signs, check PRN analgesic orders, and reassess pain intensity 30 to 60 minutes after intervention. Notify the provider if pain remains unrelieved.

### [RULE-TEMP-10] Febrile Episode & Sepsis Screening
An oral temperature of 38.0°C (100.4°F) or higher, or an increase of 1.1°C above baseline, requires a full set of vital signs (heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, SpO2) and screening for infection sources (lungs, urine, skin, indwelling catheters). Notify the physician immediately if systolic BP is below 100 mmHg or respiratory rate is 22/min or higher.

### [RULE-AGIT-11] Non-Pharmacological Dementia Agitation De-escalation
For acute restlessness, pacing, or verbal agitation in a resident with dementia without immediate physical danger, use calm redirection, reduce environmental noise, check for unmet physical needs (pain, toileting, hunger, cold), and avoid physical restraints or immediate sedative requests unless de-escalation fails.

### [RULE-GI-12] Acute Diarrhea & Enteric Isolation Precautions
Three or more loose or watery stools within 24 hours requires placing the resident on contact precautions (gloves and gown), initiating a stool output log, encouraging electrolyte replacement fluids, and notifying the provider to evaluate for infectious gastroenteritis or Clostridioides difficile.
